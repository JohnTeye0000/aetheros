# Testing the explanation for F15's failures. WRITTEN BEFORE RUNNING.
#
# Post-hoc explanation from random_mix2: interactive processes whose wake-ups
# land in the same 1 ms timer tick queue behind each other whatever the
# scheduler, so ~3% (two interactive) / ~6% (three) of wake-ups are delayed by
# one sibling's burst. If that is right:
#   C1. With NO other processes (mix2:0,0,I) every policy -- rr, mlfq, fair,
#       hint, hfair -- shows the same floor: 0% of wake-ups over 100 us for
#       I=1, 1.5-5% for I=2, 3-8% for I=3. p95 <= 100 us for I=1 and I=2,
#       > 100 us for I=3, under every policy.
#   C2. The policies' delayed fractions at a given I differ by at most 2
#       percentage points (the floor is the workload's, not the scheduler's).
# And for hint's poor random-mix latency with correct hints (0.33-2.7 ms):
#   C3. In the mixes with <= 2 interactive processes and >= 6 CPU-bound ones
#       (6;0;2 7;2;2 8;0;2 8;2;1 8;3;2) turning aging off (aging=100000) brings
#       hint's interactive p95 to <= 100 us. That would make aging (F11), not
#       ties, the cause there.
#   C4. In the three-interactive mixes (1;1;3 4;3;3 8;1;3) hint with aging off
#       stays above 100 us (the collision floor of C1), i.e. aging is not the
#       whole story there.
# Scored by experiments/collisions.py. Failures get reported as failures.
